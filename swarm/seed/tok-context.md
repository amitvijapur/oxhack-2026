## The Manh Choh mine

Manh Choh is a gold mine about 10 miles south of Tok, Alaska, and about 12 miles west of Tetlin. Manh Choh sits entirely on land of the Native Village of Tetlin, which owns the surface and mineral rights and leases them to Peak Gold LLC [DNR-IR, DNR-AR, MC-K].

Kinross holds 70% of Peak Gold LLC and operates Manh Choh. Contango ORE holds the other 30% of Peak Gold LLC [DNR-IR, DNR-AR, MC-K].

Mining at Manh Choh began in late 2023 and is expected to last 4 to 5 years. Manh Choh has about 300 employees. Manh Choh poured its first gold bar on 8 Jul 2024 and produced 141,000 ounces of gold in 2024 [DNR-IR, DNR-AR, MC-K].

Manh Choh operates under a reclamation plan approved by the Alaska Department of Natural Resources (DNR) under state law AS 27.19 [DNR-IR, DNR-AR, MC-K].

## The ore haul

Ore from Manh Choh is trucked about 240 miles one way to the Fort Knox mill near Fairbanks. The ore haul runs about 60 round trips a day. The ore trucks are B-train double trailers about 95 feet long, weighing 162,815 lb when loaded [CAP].

## Local views on the mine and the ore haul

In 2024, public meetings on the mine and the ore haul were held in Tok (30 Apr 2024), Fairbanks and Delta Junction. Of the 127 people who commented, 106 opposed the mine and the ore haul, 4 supported it and 17 gave no opinion [CAP].

The main concerns raised were pavement damage (62), maintenance costs (57), safety (55), alternatives to trucking (53) and the environment (51) [CAP]. The commenters chose to come and speak, so these numbers are not a poll of local opinion.
