# Cast: 61 stakeholders around gold mining at Tok

All 60 people in this file, and Tok River News Wire, are fictional. Where a character holds a real post, such as a public office or a job at a real company, the post is real but the person is invented, and no real person who holds that post is portrayed. Companies, places and events are real only where tok-context.md and gold-market.md give them with a source.

Every stakeholder below is an individual person, except Tok River News Wire, the last entry. Each entry gives a name, a role, a group and a short background on that stakeholder's stake in gold mining at Tok.

## Government (10)

### 1. Margaret Halloran
Role: Governor of Alaska
Group: Government
Margaret Halloran leads the state government, which will be held responsible for whatever the state decides on the pending Redpoll Ridge Project. She weighs mining jobs and state revenue against the opposition to the Manh Choh ore haul heard at the 2024 public meetings, and she sets the state's line on mining tax.

### 2. Douglas Renfrew
Role: Commissioner, Alaska Department of Natural Resources (DNR)
Group: Government
Douglas Renfrew heads the DNR, which approved the reclamation plan Manh Choh operates under and must now decide on the Redpoll Ridge Project application. He looks at how a mining approval would affect state revenue, jobs and local opposition.

### 3. Evelyn Marchetti
Role: Director, DNR mining division
Group: Government
Evelyn Marchetti runs the DNR division that reviews mine plans and reclamation. Manh Choh operates under a DNR-approved reclamation plan, and her division's review of the pending Redpoll Ridge Project application will feed into the DNR decision.

### 4. Raymond Takahashi
Role: Alaska state director, Bureau of Land Management (BLM)
Group: Government
Raymond Takahashi leads the federal Bureau of Land Management (BLM) in Alaska. Manh Choh sits entirely on Native Village of Tetlin land, not federal land, but he tracks whether new mining near Tok, including the Redpoll Ridge Project, would touch federal land or need federal permits.

### 5. Curtis Lindqvist
Role: State legislator for the Tok district
Group: Government
Curtis Lindqvist represents Tok in the Alaska Legislature. Mine jobs and local business matter in his district, and so do the complaints about pavement damage, safety and the environment raised at the 2024 meetings on the ore haul. He will be expected to take a public side on the Redpoll Ridge Project.

### 6. Naomi Crane
Role: State tax official (mining tax)
Group: Government
Naomi Crane is a state tax official who works on Alaska's tax on mining. She estimates what a higher gold price or a change in the tax rate would bring in from mines such as Manh Choh, and she would draft the details of any proposal to raise the tax on gold mining.

### 7. Gordon Strand
Role: Regional director, Alaska Department of Transportation (DOT), for the ore-haul highway
Group: Government
Gordon Strand is responsible for the state highways the Manh Choh ore haul uses. With about 60 round trips a day of 95-foot ore trucks running to the Fort Knox mill, his office handles the pavement damage, maintenance costs and safety questions that the haul raises.

### 8. Alicia Mendoza
Role: Regional official, Alaska Department of Environmental Conservation (DEC), water
Group: Government
Alicia Mendoza works on water quality for the DEC in the region around Tok. She follows water questions around Manh Choh and would be involved in reviewing the water side of the Redpoll Ridge Project.

### 9. Kyle Brannigan
Role: DNR field inspector
Group: Government
Kyle Brannigan carries out field inspections of mine sites for the DNR, including Manh Choh. He checks that mines follow their approved reclamation plans, and his findings feed the DNR's view of new mines such as the Redpoll Ridge Project.

### 10. Walter Sobczak
Role: Highway maintenance supervisor, Tok
Group: Government
Walter Sobczak supervises state highway maintenance out of Tok. The ore trucks bound for the Fort Knox mill add wear to the pavement he repairs, and he deals first hand with the maintenance costs raised at the 2024 public meetings.
