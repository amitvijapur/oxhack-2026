## User thesis

Use approximately five years of commodity and relevant mineral-property prices to establish a baseline. Combine spatial multi-element geological evidence with property characteristics to understand mineral potential and relative valuation. Seven simulations under different news conditions describe potential stakeholder reactions. Those reactions may help forecast commodity price changes and local demand for particular properties. Forecast the resulting contract value distribution, compare with available acquisition terms and size positions within available contracts and capital.

The desired output is predictive and ultimately tradeable mineral exposure through contracts. A cash-flow calculator alone does not satisfy the request. Five years currently describes the preferred lookback, not an agreed forecast horizon.

## Relationships to resolve

1. Geological measurements to a defined geological target, such as mineralisation probability or relative attractiveness. Sample concentration, resource quantity and recoverable metal are different quantities.
2. Historical comparable interests to an estimated current value and future transaction outcome. Asking prices, announced consideration, obligations and completed payments need separate meanings.
3. News and simulated stakeholder responses to target-specific changes in gold prices, buyer demand, bids, delays or costs. Seven scenarios do not supply seven probabilities by themselves.
4. Commodity changes and local effects to the same property's repricing. Avoid entering a single gold-price move twice through baseline and sentiment adjustments.
5. Property/interest forecasts to the actual contract's cash flows, exercise/assignment rules and feasible exit. Forecasting precedes action classification and sizing.

Two proposed channels should remain distinguishable: global commodity exposure and local demand/permission/liquidity effects. Static geological information may already be reflected in the entry price. The source of a prospective edge must be specified, such as better interpretation, earlier detection or an unpriced interaction.

## Existing evidence and limits

- Current Tok evidence comprises 67 stream-sediment samples and claim context. It does not establish recoverable ounces or a validated price mapping. The [DGGS source report](https://dggs.alaska.gov/webpubs/dggs/rdf/text/rdf2016_010.pdf) describes a programme assessing mineral-resource potential, including streams draining known occurrences.
- The existing 40-row agreement catalogue records heterogeneous terms, not a demonstrated five-year repeat-sales series. New sources may change this assessment.
- Seven simulated conditions and stakeholder agreement are model outputs. They are not observed future market consensus or calibrated probabilities without evaluation.
- The [CIMVAL code](https://mrmr.cim.org/media/1147/cimval-code-november2019_may2022.pdf) distinguishes property stages and valuation approaches. Exact interest and development stage matter when selecting comparables and economic models.
- Mineral rights and surface rights must be identified in the actual instrument. For example, [BLM guidance](https://www.blm.gov/programs/energy-and-minerals/mining-and-minerals/locatable-minerals/mining-claims) says federal mining claims do not include exclusive surface rights; this is not a determination about any proposed Alaska state interest.

### Holding-horizon proposal, not yet confirmed

Amit suggested weeks to months and asked which horizon fits news and swarm signals. Proposed research target: potential assignment within 90 calendar days, comparing 30 and 180 days. This is a modelling choice to test, not an observed optimal holding period or a demonstrated Tok resale time. Model both assignment proceeds conditional on a sale and the probability/timing of sale, with an explicit unsold branch. Do not force a liquid exit on day 90.

The pinned swarm plan covers five simulated days and measures stakeholder support and day-five gold views. Its simulated clock does not establish real forecast skill or persistence through a 90-day holding period. Distinguish transient sentiment from changes to rights, funding, access or credible geological evidence that might have more persistent valuation consequences. The model must test decay/retention and later observable outcomes rather than multiply a five-day effect across three months.

Contract expiry should leave room after the intended resale horizon for actual transfer/closing requirements. Six to twelve months remaining could be a hypothetical contract-search specification, not evidence such options exist or that their premiums are attractive. Exact buffer and obligations await a real contract and buyer process.
