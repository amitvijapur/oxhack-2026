## 1. Evaluate three different claims separately

**Evidence.** Proper scoring rules compare a forecast with the event or value that subsequently occurs. Simulation verification instead checks implementation; operational validation concerns accuracy for the intended real system. Internal consistency is useful evidence about a simulator but does not establish external forecasting accuracy. [Gneiting and Raftery, 2007](https://doi.org/10.1198/016214506000001437); [Sargent, 2013](https://ics-websites.science.uu.nl/docs/vakken/msosi/VerificationValidationSargent.pdf).

**Proposed replacement for the mixed labels at lines 383–386:**

| Component | Target and ground truth | Evaluation and baseline | Permitted conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|
| Claims activity | New filings or a predeclared burst within one cell during the next 12 months; authoritative dated records and reporting coverage | Rolling-origin tests; count error against trailing-count/seasonal count, or Brier score against historical burst frequency if predicting an event probability | Skill at predicting the specified recorded activity |
| Contract return | Currency-denominated net cash flows for a specified right, entry price, costs and horizon; verified transaction terms and observed exit/cash flows | Later transactions and held-out properties; dollar error, interval coverage and net utility against a frozen simple policy | Monetary predictive/decision value for that measured population |
| Hypothetical decision | Payoffs generated by the declared scenario and its assumptions | Hand calculations or exact enumeration, then policies on identical seeded worlds; report utility, cost, deadline misses and assumption sensitivity | Correct conditional scenario calculations |

A claim transfer without a price, or later staking nearby, cannot supply a missing contract-return observation. Keep it in the activity target. If exit value is unobserved, record it as missing/censored; do not invent a sale, mark-to-model profit or zero return. Five-day stakeholder agreement belongs in a separate consistency/grounding table, not any of these accuracy scores. These are proposed target definitions, inferred from the available fields.

**Chronology proposal.** Store `event_time`, `first_available_at`, `retrieved_at`, source version/hash and timestamp precision. Build each feature from the vintage available at the decision time. Only train on labels resolved and available by that training cutoff; split or exclude overlapping outcome windows across train/test. Fit transformations, selection and calibration inside training periods. ALFRED explicitly distinguishes observation dates from the historical vintage available to a user. [St. Louis Fed documentation](https://alfred.stlouisfed.org/help/downloaddata).

For geography, hold out complete districts/properties and report interpolation and transfer separately. Spatial blocking addresses dependence but can deliberately create extrapolation, so its design must match the intended use. An NV/AZ test cannot establish Alaska performance without Alaska outcomes; partial pooling is a modelling assumption. [Roberts et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1111/ecog.02881).

Freezing an old prompt date does not remove a modern LLM's later knowledge. Treat historical swarm replays as potentially contaminated unless the model's information boundary is defensible. Prospective forecasts with immutable submissions and later resolution avoid that particular problem, as designed in [ForecastBench, ICLR 2025](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/ea74e45a229dac70b5b63b28d8934db6-Paper-Conference.pdf).

## 2. Replace automatic precision fusion with an explicit experiment

**Evidence.** The cited Black–Litterman derivation has views of expected returns, normal error models, specified uncertainty and view errors independent of the prior. Its appendix states those assumptions explicitly. It does not justify blending unlike outcomes or discovering confidence from agent agreement. [He and Litterman, 1999, Appendix B](https://www.cis.upenn.edu/~mkearns/finread/intuition.pdf).

**Mathematical check.** For independent unbiased Gaussian measurements of the same scalar target, with variances `v_q` and `v_s`, the combined estimate is `(mu_q/v_q + mu_s/v_s)/(1/v_q + 1/v_s)`. If errors have covariance `c`, the minimum-variance linear weight on the swarm becomes `(v_q-c)/(v_q+v_s-2c)`, subject to a coherent positive-definite covariance model. These are algebraic results, not estimates of this project's errors. The prior's uncertainty in its mean is also distinct from the variability of future returns.

`run_std²/K` describes the sampling uncertainty of an independent sample mean conditional on its generator. The proposed view is a median, and repeated seeds share prompts, evidence and models. Neither case automatically supplies that variance. Shared systematic errors remain even when reruns agree. Kim et al. find substantial correlated errors across LLMs; their benchmark result motivates caution but does not measure correlation in this swarm. [ICML 2025 paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/kim25e.html).

**Proposed changes to lines 229–242:**

- Require identical target, region, as-of information, horizon and units before any fusion. Do not combine a gold return directly with land value or claim counts.
- Keep the solver's empirical baseline independent of the swarm. Initially use swarm outputs only as labelled stress scenarios; calibrated fusion weight is disabled.
- Report rerun medians/ranges as descriptive stability. Never shrink economic uncertainty merely by adding seeds or treating shock runs as additional baseline observations.
- If useful later, test fixed influence settings such as 0%, 10% and 25% as **assumed sensitivity settings**, not calibrated posterior weights. The current 25% ceiling is a governance choice.
- Enable learned weights only after target-matched resolved cases support out-of-sample bias/error, covariance, calibration and incremental-value comparisons. Twenty cases is not a universal sufficiency threshold. Select calibrators within training data and report uncertainty.

## 3. Proposed five-day interface

The following schema is a proposal for Muhd and Salman, not a verified MiroFish export. One record represents one scenario run:

```text
schema_version, run_id, variant, seed
region_id, as_of_utc, generated_at_utc
evidence_refs[{source_id, record_id, first_available_at, hash}]
provenance{model_versions, prompt_hash, config_hash, runtime_version}
target{instrument_id, reference_source, start_utc, end_utc,
       return_definition: "P(end)/P(start)-1", horizon_days: 5}
status: "hypothetical_scenario"
gold_return_fraction: number|null
policy_scenarios[{event_id, proposed_event, onset_day, evidence_refs}]
daily_group_sentiment[{day, group, label, supporting_post_refs}]
unknowns[], unsupported_claims[], extraction_errors[]
```

Validate IDs, horizon, references, finite values and units; `0.05` means +5%. Missing information stays null. No `p_up`, policy-event probability or claimed confidence interval is required until there is a defined estimation/calibration method. A generated stakeholder expectation is not an observed market price. A future scoring contract must freeze the exact price source, endpoint convention, event thresholds and resolution time first; monthly gold data cannot resolve a five-day return.

**Mapping proposal.** The adapter freezes a reviewed `scenario_id` containing actual contract terms where available, and separately identified assumptions: entry/strike/premium, fees, deadlines, baseline land-value scenarios and permitted actions. Map each swarm event only through an explicit assumption record, for example a specified delay added to time-to-close. Preserve the source event and the human-approved mapping. A proposed tax debate is not an enacted tax cash flow.

Apply the gold scenario only within its five-day horizon. Keep the contract horizon separately stated. Any continuation beyond day five must explicitly define and test retention, decay or reversion of the resulting price-level change. Resuming the same return dynamics from a shifted day-five price can retain that shift indefinitely; it is not an assumption of zero persistence. Without a continuation mapping, leave the longer-horizon swarm effect unknown and do not propagate it into monetary valuation. The independent no-swarm baseline remains available. Never annualise the five-day view into multi-year expected land appreciation. A gold-to-land response requires its own fitted model or labelled assumed elasticity; without one, no dollar land-value adjustment is made.

Compare identical contracts/worlds with and without each assumed shock. Display conditional action changes and drivers, retaining a working no-swarm path. This lets the demo expose time sensitivity while empirical calibration remains unresolved.
